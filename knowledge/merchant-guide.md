## What the role is

The official BatMUD guild page describes the Master Merchants as an economic and social guild with maximum level 25. Entry requires either a Civilized background or maximum level in the Channellers guild. The page also exposes level-by-level requirements and skill/spell caps. BatWiki's guild table lists a broad toolkit, including Bargain, Shopkeeping, Exchange Money, Money is Power, Remote Banking, Appraise, Identify, Mining, Minecrafting, crafting, repair, and other trade skills. This suggests more than one career: market services and resale, merchant infrastructure/crafting, or high-level mining/adventure content.

The live game's `help merchant` redirected to `merchants` and describes the guild as weak in melee and powerful offensive spells, with its strength in money and production. It lists crafting/protecting weapons and armour, building chests/ships/player-city rooms, plastic surgery, featherweighting, minting coins, gathering raw materials through lumberjacking/mining/skinning, a quest-unlocked Toolbelt, and merchant carts/stores. The capture stops at 55% because the pager did not return the next page; see [in-game capture](../sources/in-game-help/2026-10-22-merchants.md).

## Practical expectations

- Merchant activities can produce money, but require market knowledge, time online when customers need services, and often a starting pool of capital.
- The official player library's *Guide to Succesful Merchanting* (Finaldey, 1996) describes Money is Power (MiP) and money exchange services, cart-based resale, bargain-assisted purchases, and pricing against competition. It explicitly cautions that merchants do not necessarily make money quickly.
- The guide's exact targets (experience thresholds, skill percentages, discounts, fees, cart price caps, and named shop locations/items) are from 1996 and should be treated as historical examples, not current instructions.
- The 2023 *Batmud Guide to Earning Gold*, last modified 2026-08-24, argues that merchant-specific income loops demand substantial experience and social/market connections and may be less accessible to new characters. It recommends ordinary combat income as a more practical early-game source for players who mainly need starter gold. This is one player's guide, not a universal rule.
- A newer player write-up, *Adventuring in Merchant Mineshafts* (published 2026-09-07, updated 2026-09-10), describes merchant-created mines as an indirect earning path: merchants create mines, while adventurers explore them; the author says merchants receive experience when visitors explore and kill monsters. It also describes portals, cleared-mine debris searches, and risks from aggressive wandering monsters and respawns. Treat all procedures and rewards as player observations that need live help and character-appropriate difficulty checks.

2. For merchant entry, select Civilized background, or meet the alternate requirement of maximum Channellers level. Background/current guild levels are not shown in the captured `score`; eligibility must be checked before committing.
3. Race shortlist: Hobbit is the practical provisional merchant candidate; Sprite is a higher-INT/WIS but costly and fragile alternative; Drow is a high-WIS but costly option. Compare the [race table](../research/race-comparison.md) and choose based on the user's preference as well as stats.
4. Character fit: current Charisma 100 supports Bargain, Shopkeeping, and Exchange Money, each documented as Charisma-based. Train toward those market-facing services after checking current merchant guild level gates and experience costs. MiP depends on Wisdom 47 and consumes all carried money on its target to award experience; keep valuable cash secured if ever interacting with it.
5. Read current in-game `help money`, `help skill bargain`, `help skill shopkeeping`, `help skill exchange money`, `help spell money is power`, and cart help before operating any economic loop. Their current details are summarized in [merchant skill and spell help](../sources/in-game-help/2026-10-22-merchant-skills.md); the Exchange Money page is incomplete.
6. Before buying inventory for resale, inspect current sales listings and cart/shop rules, then start with low-cost items whose identity, condition, and likely resale price are known. Track purchase cost, time to sell, fees, and unsold stock.

The recent mineshaft guide expands the merchant playstyle beyond selling: creating adventure mines may let a merchant support exploration and gain experience indirectly, though current prerequisites, costs, and reward formulas are not confirmed.
